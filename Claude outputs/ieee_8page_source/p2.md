
# II. Methodology

## A. Problem Formulation

A payer is about to authorise a push payment that has not yet been submitted:

$$x = \{u,\, p,\, a,\, t,\, q,\, m,\, l\} \qquad (1)$$

where $u$ is the payer, $p$ the payee address (a VPA, a mobile number or an address extracted from a QR payload), $a$ the amount, $t$ the assessment time, $q$ the raw QR payload, $m$ the message that prompted the payment and $l$ the links it carries. Every component except $p$ and $t$ may be absent. Let $\mathcal{H}_{<t}$ be the payment observations recorded strictly before $t$, from which the payer profile, the pair history of $(u,p)$, the payee history of $p$ and the payer–payee graph $\mathcal{G}_{<t}$ are derived. Settlement outcomes and the fraud label are unavailable at $t$. The system computes a risk score $R(x)\in\{0,\dots,99\}$, a coverage score $C(x)\in\{0,\dots,100\}$ and an advisory verdict $V(x)\in\{$APPROVE, WARN, STEP_UP, BLOCK$\}$, together with human-readable findings.

Four constraints shape the design: (i) nothing may depend on information after $t$; (ii) an unreadable source must not make the assessment more favourable, nor (iii) by itself less favourable; and (iv) one observation reported by several components is not independent corroboration.

## B. Architecture and Workflow

The system comprises a client, an assessment service and a store (Fig. 1). The client decodes a QR code or accepts a typed address and optionally collects the amount, a stated purpose and the prompting message; the service evaluates and aggregates ten evidence families; the store holds an observation ledger with provenance labels, per-payee reputation aggregates, the incremental graph and payer profiles. The *assessment path* only reads the store. The *observation path* writes a payment — on the payer's confirmation or on statement import — updating ledger, aggregates and graph in one database transaction, so the request being assessed cannot influence the state against which it is assessed. The prototype uses a Python service, scikit-learn and SQLite.

## C. Evidence Families and Availability

Each family $i$ returns an evidence record $e_i=(a_i, z_i, \Gamma_i, \Phi_i)$: an availability flag, a score, findings and a set of *facts* drawn from a closed vocabulary of fourteen observations (e.g., HOUR_ANOMALY, PAYEE_SHAPE, GRAPH_POSITION, MESSAGE_COERCION). Facts name *what* was observed independently of *which* family observed it. Availability and score satisfy

$$a_i=1 \Rightarrow z_i\in\{0,\dots,100\},\qquad a_i=0 \Rightarrow z_i=\bot,\ \Phi_i=\emptyset \qquad (2)$$

so "looked and found nothing" is distinct from "could not look". Table I lists the families. A never-observed but readable address keeps the payee-history family available with a low-weight "unseen payee" finding (6 points), which alone stays below the WARN band; an unreadable family contributes nothing.

## D. Incremental Payer–Payee Graph

Each confirmed payment $u\to p$ increments a directed edge carrying a count and first/last timestamps, updates a per-payee record (distinct payers $N_p$, repeat payers, payment count, first and last arrival) and merges the components of $u$ and $p$ in a union–find structure, all in the transaction that records the payment. The assessment path reads the payee record with one indexed lookup and the peer sharing most payers with $p$ through a bounded two-hop query. With $P(p)$ the payer set of $p$ in $\mathcal{G}_{<t}$, the overlap with a peer $p'$ is

$$\omega(p,p')=\frac{|P(p)\cap P(p')|}{\min(|P(p)|,|P(p')|)} \qquad (3)$$

Let $p^\star$ maximise the shared count and $r_p$ be the share of $p$'s payers who paid more than once. A *position* finding (fact GRAPH_POSITION) fires only if both addresses have at least ten payers, $|P(p)\cap P(p^\star)|\ge4$, $\omega(p,p^\star)\ge0.6$ and $r_p<0.1$: a pattern consistent with collection accounts sharing one victim pool rather than with merchants, whose customers return. *Shape* findings (many one-time payers, a compressed arrival window) restate payee history and therefore carry zero weight and the PAYEE_SHAPE fact. Component size is recorded but is never a finding. The graph is explicit analytics: it is not a graph neural network, performs no dense-subgraph peeling {{spade}} and learns no representation {{pgltse}}.

## E. Calibrated Risk Model

The classifier is a histogram-based gradient-boosting model {{friedman}}, {{lightgbm}} (300 iterations, learning rate 0.08, 31 leaves, balanced class weights) wrapped in five-fold isotonic calibration {{niculescu}}, {{sklearn}}. Class weighting replaces resampling; no SMOTE {{smote}} is used, and calibration returns the probabilities to the observed scale. Missing inputs are left missing and routed natively. The threshold $\theta$ is the value with the highest validation recall subject to a validation false-positive rate (FPR) of at most 1%. The calibrated probability $\pi$ is mapped to a bounded family score

$$z_{\mathrm{ml}}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{round}\,g(\pi;0,\theta,0,18), & \pi<\theta\\ \mathrm{round}\,g(\pi;\theta,0.8,30,66), & \pi\ge\theta\end{cases} \qquad (4)$$

where $g(v;v_0,v_1,y_0,y_1)$ maps $[v_0,v_1]$ linearly onto $[y_0,y_1]$ with clipping. The ceiling of 66 keeps the classifier alone below BLOCK. Facts are attributed to the classifier counterfactually: a fact is declared only if neutralising its inputs lowers $\pi$ by at least $\max(0.01, 0.15\pi)$.

## F. Evidence Aggregation and Verdict

Let $z_{(1)}\ge\dots\ge z_{(n)}$ be the scores of *active* families ($a_i=1$, $z_i>0$). The strongest signal leads and further signals are damped:

$$R_0=\min\!\left(99,\ \mathrm{round}\!\left(z_{(1)}+0.4\sum_{j\ge2}z_{(j)}\right)\right) \qquad (5)$$

Let $\mathcal{A}^\star$ be the families with $z_i\ge20$ that declare facts and $\Phi^\star$ the union of their facts. Corroboration counts both streams and observations:

$$k=\min(|\Phi^\star|,|\mathcal{A}^\star|),\qquad R=\min\!\left(99,\,R_0+B(k)\right) \qquad (6)$$

with $B(k)=0$ for $k<2$ and $B=6,14,22,28,32$ for $k=2,\dots,\ge6$. Counting families prevents one family from corroborating itself, and counting distinct facts prevents families that read one observation from appearing to agree. Coverage depends only on availability,

$$C=\mathrm{round}\!\left(100\sum_i a_i c_i\big/\sum_i c_i\right) \qquad (7)$$

and an evidence level is FULL when payee history, payer behaviour and relationship are all available, and PARTIAL or MINIMAL otherwise. With $\mathcal{B}$ the hard-block findings (blocked payee, malformed address, non-payment payload, amount above the rail limit),

$$V=\begin{cases}\text{BLOCK}, & \Gamma\cap\mathcal{B}\ne\emptyset \text{ or } R\ge70\\ \text{STEP\_UP}, & 45\le R\le69\\ \text{WARN}, & 22\le R\le44\\ \text{APPROVE}, & R\le21\end{cases} \qquad (8)$$

The bands are fixed design parameters. The verdicts are advisory states of one score, not ground-truth classes, and no verdict holds or reverses a payment. Each response lists its findings, the facts that participated in corroboration with the families that reported them, and the families that could not be evaluated, with reasons; these findings are the user-facing explanation (Fig. 2).
