
# III. Experimentation

## A. Synthetic Data

Labelled UPI fraud data held by banks, PSPs and NPCI are not publicly released, and none were used. Two synthetic corpora in the tradition of agent-based payment simulators {{paysim}} are used instead (Table II); their scenarios, overlaps and channel probabilities were specified by the authors, so every result characterises controlled synthetic conditions.

*Row-level corpus (Experiments 1–2).* Each seed generates 120,000 payments from 2,000 payers at a nominal fraud rate of 0.012 (seed 42: 1,419 fraudulent, 1.183%) with a fixed mixture of social engineering (SE) 50%, mule collection (MC) 22%, account takeover (AT) 20% and card testing (CT) 8%. Each row is the evidence state just before one payment, sampled from scenario-conditional distributions that deliberately overlap with legitimate behaviour; payee history is hidden on 24.48% of rows (seed 42). The V2 relationship, graph, message, link and intent channels are simulator-generated, with messages drawn from a pool of 1,022 templates (364 scam, 557 hard and 101 easy negatives) written for the project.

*Replay streams (Experiments 3–4).* Because the row-level corpus has no identities or clock, identity-bearing streams were generated for seeds 42–44: 60,000 time-ordered payments over 150 days by 2,000 payers to 900 merchants (Zipf-like popularity, 30% opening during the window) and to fraud-controlled addresses, with 720 fraudulent payments each (SE 360, MC 158, AT 144, CT 58). SE collectors receive Poisson(7)+2 victims, 35% through an aged address; MC rings of 3–4 accounts collect from 20–45 victims, 60% of whom pay two ring accounts. Payer behaviour and the message, link and intent channels reuse the row-level generators and template pool.

## B. Protocol and Metrics

For every corpus, 25% of rows are held out as test and 20% of the remainder as validation, stratified on the label with the seed as random state. Models are fitted on training rows only; $\theta$ is chosen on validation (FPR ≤ 1%) and applied once to the test split. PR-AUC (average precision) is the primary metric because it reflects alert dilution under imbalance {{saito}}, {{davis}}; ROC-AUC is secondary. At $\theta$ we report recall, realised FPR, precision, F1 and per-scenario recall, and the Brier score with its skill score (BSS) against the base-rate predictor {{brier}}.

## C. Experiments 1–2: Stability and Ablation

*Ten-seed stability.* The whole pipeline — generation, split, fitting, calibration, threshold selection, test measurement — is repeated for seeds 42–51. The seed is the unit of replication: statistics are summarised as mean ± sample sd, minimum and maximum over ten seed-level values; test sets are never pooled and no seed is selected.

*Feature-family ablation.* Eleven configurations use the same seeds, splits, learner and threshold rule: A, V1; B, a 12-feature V2 *core* (payer timing, amount, velocity, payee); C–G, core plus one of relationship, graph, message, link or intent; H, core + relationship + graph; I, H + message; J, I + link; K, full V2. Differences are paired by seed and given in percentage points (pp). Because train and test messages share one template pool on which the coercion model was fitted, a *template-disjoint control* repeats E, I and K after splitting the templates 50/50 within each kind, refitting the coercion model on the training half, and giving test rows only held-out templates.

## D. Experiments 3–4: End-to-End Evaluation and Graph Replay

Each replay stream is processed in time order (Fig. 3). For each payment the clock is set to its time $t$; the payer's prior payments and the shared store, which holds only earlier payments, form the pre-payment state; the production assessment function behind the service's check endpoint returns risk, coverage, level, verdict, per-family evidence and findings; and only then is the payment recorded through the production observation path. The live classifier family uses the stored V1 artefact without refitting. The only runtime substitution is the injected clock, because payee age is computed from the application's "now".

*End-to-end metrics.* Continuous risk is evaluated with PR-AUC and ROC-AUC, separately from the verdict mix, which is reported as an intervention profile and as operating points of $R$ — not as classes. The primary slice is the last 25% of each stream (days ≈112–150); 30-day blocks show the effect of store maturity. Per family we report activation by label, the within-available AUC of its score and a leave-one-family-out counterfactual that re-applies (5)–(8). Every recorded output is also recomputed from the recorded evidence as a consistency audit.

*Graph replay tests.* A plain-Python shadow model of all earlier payments is checked at every read: (1) a first pair is new; (2) the prior pair count, (3) distinct payers, (4) the arrival window and (5) the connected component equal the shadow; (6) a positional finding fires exactly when its gates hold; (7) an unobserved payee has *unavailable*, not zero, graph evidence; and (10) the store holds no payment at or after $t$. Afterwards, (8) an independent process rebuilds a fresh store from earlier payments only and recomputes the full assessment for up to 60 stratified payments per stream, and (9) every observation written before the stream midpoint must be unchanged at the end. The predictive value of replayed graph features is measured on a chronological 60/15/25 split with four variants: A, V1 from the replayed state; B, V2 with the graph columns replaced by the row-level simulator's graph channel (a contrast only, since that channel is conditioned on the true scenario); C, V2 with replayed graph features; D, V2 without graph features.

![Fig. 3. Chronological replay. For each payment at time t: set the clock, read state before t, assess with the production function, record the evidence, cross-check against a shadow model, and only then record the payment. Tests 8–9 are run afterwards.](fig/fig3_replay.png){width=3.35in}

A leakage audit grades, from artefacts and recomputation, split disjointness for every seed, validation-only threshold selection, the absence of test-derived preprocessing, the pre-payment boundary of every replayed assessment (tests 1–10) and message-template overlap as PASS, FAIL or WARNING.
